Two ways to carry more than one year

Both prototypes read the real bundle and then pad it with dated copies of the 2026 filings, so the rail and the board are under the pressure of 35 deals across 5 years rather than 9 in one. Open each, switch years, search, and see which way of moving feels right. Nothing here changes the live dashboard.

  • Scope control at the top of the rail sets the year for the whole page.
  • Search filters the rail live; / focuses it, and it can reach across all years.
  • Group by year, sector or stage — collapsible headers keep the list short.
  • Compare years is its own view, since a single-year rail cannot show the trend.
  • Rail collapses to a 60px strip with [.

Best if you mostly work one year at a time and want the current dashboard to keep feeling like itself. Lowest-risk change — the main pane is untouched.

The year key, as decided

A filing belongs to the book of its filing year — the date it was first submitted to the SEC. Both prototypes read sec_status.first_filed, falling back to the earliest document the SEC published for that filing, then to the newest document date. ALPHART is the only filing that needs the fallback today: it is not on the SEC list yet, so it lands in 2026 on its 31 Aug 2026 documents. Where both dates exist the earliest document always agrees with first_filed on the year, so the fallback never moves a filing between books.

What this changes: the current book splits. QHHRREIT (8 Sep 2025) and MII (18 Dec 2025) move into a 2025 book, leaving seven filings worth THB 18.83bn in 2026 rather than nine worth THB 21.52bn. Worth knowing before the workbook and the folder — both named 2026 — follow suit.
